# Essential Hypertension — Diagnosis and Monitoring

Illustrative synthetic guidance written for a demo. Not medical advice.

## Diagnosis
Hypertension is diagnosed when clinic blood pressure is ≥ 140/90 mmHg on two or more separate visits, ideally confirmed by home or ambulatory monitoring. The diagnosis date is the visit at which it was confirmed.

## Medical Necessity of Monitoring
After diagnosis or a medication change, review every 4–6 weeks until controlled, then every 3–6 months. Monitoring includes blood pressure measurement, electrolytes and creatinine, and screening for target-organ damage (ECG, urine albumin). Regular monitoring prevents stroke, heart failure and kidney disease.

## Chronic but Newly Diagnosed
Hypertension is lifelong once diagnosed. However, the clinical record of first diagnosis determines when the disease began. A first diagnosis made after a policy started means the disease was not present in the record before that date.

# Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus — Monitoring and Follow-up

Illustrative synthetic guidance written for a demo. Not medical advice.

## Diagnosis
Type 2 diabetes is diagnosed by fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL, HbA1c ≥ 6.5%, or a 2-hour glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL on oral glucose tolerance testing. The date of the first confirmed diagnostic test is the date of diagnosis recorded in the medical record.

## Prediabetes
Prediabetes (HbA1c 5.7–6.4% or fasting glucose 100–125 mg/dL) needs yearly glucose testing and lifestyle counselling to delay progression to type 2 diabetes. Follow-up consultations for prediabetes are preventive care and are medically necessary.

## Medical Necessity of Follow-up
Regular follow-up is the standard of care. HbA1c should be measured every 3 months until glycaemic targets are met and every 6 months thereafter. Each follow-up visit should review glucose control, blood pressure, lipids, kidney function (eGFR, urine albumin) and foot and eye screening. Missing follow-up raises the risk of retinopathy, nephropathy and cardiovascular events.

## Newly Diagnosed Disease
A patient whose diabetes is first detected after enrolling in a health plan has a NEW diagnosis on that date. Chronicity describes the course of the disease. It does not make the disease pre-existing to a policy that began before the diagnosis.
